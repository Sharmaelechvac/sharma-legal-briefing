1. Big legal news (federal)

1.1 SCOTUS grants cert in Mullin v. RAICES — Fri Nov 13, 2026

  • What happened: The Supreme Court granted the government's petition in Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services (RAICES), No. 26-239, but limited review to two of the three questions presented: (1) whether Proclamation 10888 ("Guaranteeing the States Protection Against Invasion," Jan 20, 2025) and its implementing guidance — which suspend asylum access for people crossing the southern border illegally — are lawful; and (2) whether 8 U.S.C. §1252(f)(1) bars classwide relief. The D.D.C. set the policy aside as unlawful (Jul 2, 2025, Judge Moss); the D.C. Circuit largely upheld that ruling (Apr 24, 2026; merits decision at 174 F.4th 81). The appeals court stayed its ruling, so the ban remains in effect during review. Argument is expected in spring 2027, decision by end of June 2027. Note: this is a different Mullin case from the Nov 9 stay item in yesterday's briefing — the Nov 13 cert grant is new.
  • Why it matters generally: It will define the outer limit of presidential power under INA §212(f) to override congressionally created asylum procedures by proclamation, and the availability of classwide injunctions against immigration policies.
  • Sharma impact: No change to Sharma's I-9/E-Verify duties. Indirect: continued restriction of a labour pipeline Florida construction draws on, and a signal that federal immigration enforcement posture stays aggressive through 2027 — keep hiring documentation airtight (see §3.4).
  • Sources (reputable reporting + party release; grant also on Court's docket): Reuters, Nov 13 — https://www.reuters.com/world/supreme-court-will-weigh-trumps-asylum-restrictions-us-mexico-border-2026-11-13/ ; Mealey's, Nov 13, 3:18 PM EST — https://www.mealeys.com/mealeys/articles/2550093/u-s-supreme-court-will-hear-2-questions-in-noncitizen-removal-class-case ; ACLU release, Nov 13 — https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/supreme-court-to-hear-challenge-to-trump-proclamation-eliminating-asylum ; DOJ petition (primary filing) — https://www.justice.gov/osg/media/1464411/dl?inline

1.3 Acima rent-to-own settlement — $183.5M, 43 states including Florida — announced Thu Nov 12 – Fri Nov 13, 2026

  • What happened: 43 state AGs, including Florida's, settled with Acima Digital LLC and Acima Holdings LLC for $183.5M: $43M consumer restitution, $10.5M to the states, and $130M in lease balance reductions. The states alleged Acima marketed "90-day payoff" rental-purchase agreements without disclosing that consumers were automatically defaulted into the maximum 12-month term (costing roughly twice the retail partner's cash price), deceptively marked up cash prices, obscured e-signature terms, obstructed returns/termination, and engaged in deceptive debt collection and credit reporting. Injunctive terms include a 5-year ban on reporting to the big-three credit bureaus, retail-partner training, and clear cost disclosures. Eligible consumers need take no action. Releases were issued Nov 12 (e.g., NJ, CT AGs) and Nov 13 (e.g., DE, AZ, NE AGs); no separate Florida AG release was located.
  • Why it matters generally: The largest multistate consumer-financing settlement of the season, and it was state-led after federal enforcement was dropped — states are now the primary consumer-credit cops.
  • Sharma impact: Direct template risk. If Sharma offers, arranges, or co-markets any consumer financing for HVAC replacements (a common practice), the Acima fact pattern is the checklist regulators will apply: total-cost disclosure at signing, no auto-rollover into longer terms, honest cash-price comparisons, easy cancellation/return, and clean credit reporting. Florida's participation means FDUTPA exposure sits behind the same conduct.
  • Sources (PRIMARY — state AG releases): Delaware AG, Nov 13 — https://news.delaware.gov/2026/11/13/ag-jennings-announces-183-5-million-multistate-settlement-with-rent-to-own-finance-company-acima-digital-llc/ ; NJ AG, Nov 12 — https://wpadmin-prd.njoag.gov/attorney-general-davenport-announces-183-5-million-multistate-settlement-with-rent-to-own-finance-company-acima-digital-llc/

1.4 DOJ "English-only" guidance published in the Federal Register — Thu Nov 12, 2026

  • What happened: DOJ published the Attorney General memorandum "Guidance on Implementing English as the Official Language" (91 FR 72923, Doc. No. 2026-22966, Docket No. OLP188), implementing Executive Order 14224 (Mar 1, 2025), which revoked Clinton-era EO 13166 on language access. Agencies are told to "strive to operate solely in English" across correspondence, regulations, notices, forms, websites, outreach, internal communications, and AI tools; multilingual assistance only where required by law or necessary to the agency's mission, with a disclaimer that English is official. Public comments are invited through Jan 11, 2027; the guidance states it creates no enforceable rights, and DOJ has described it as already in effect.
  • Why it matters generally: A government-wide shift that will strip Spanish-language versions from federal forms and portals over time.
  • Sharma impact: Practical, not legal: Spanish versions of OSHA, IRS, USCIS, and SAM.gov materials the company's bilingual crews and office staff rely on may disappear. Sharma's own duties are unchanged — OSHA still requires training in a language/format workers understand, and Title VII obligations are unaffected. Budget for in-house translation of key federal forms.
  • Source (PRIMARY — Federal Register notice): https://thefederalregister.org/documents/2026-22966/attorney-general-memorandum-on-implementing-english-as-the-official-language-request-for-information ; reporting, NY Post, Nov 13 — https://nypost.com/2026/11/13/us-news/trump-admin-pushes-federal-agencies-to-go-english-only-in-sweeping-new-directive/

1.6 Eleventh Circuit revives Florida construction FCA qui tam suit — reported Thu Nov 12 – Fri Nov 13, 2026

  • What happened: The Eleventh Circuit disposed of the appeal in U.S. ex rel. Berry & Gose v. Native American Services Corp. and Great American Insurance Group Inc., sending the False Claims Act suit back to the Middle District of Florida.   • Sharma impact: If Sharma ever subs on federal, set-aside, or 8(a)/DBE-adjacent work — directly or under a prime — its own employees are potential relators and its certifications are FCA exposure, with treble damages. 2. Florida

2.1 Florida Supreme Court rules amendments — Thu Nov 12, 2026

The Court's only listed actions on Nov 12 were rules amendments: - Rule 1.820 (non-binding arbitration), No. SC2026-0040 — effective immediately. Eliminates filing the arbitration decision and transcripts under seal with the clerk; substitutes the arbitrator's notice of service; whether an arbitration award stands now hinges only on a timely request for trial de novo; new subdivision (i) sets deadlines for filing the decision after judgment to enforce §44.103, Fla. Stat. - Rules 1.530 / Small Claims 7.180 / Family Law 12.530 (post-judgment motions), No. SC2025-1795 — effective Jan 1, 2027. New-trial and rehearing motions are split into separate subdivisions; "new trial" is confined to actions tried by jury to verdict; "rehearing" is expressly limited to a final judgment or final order; the preservation/"necessity of motion" rules are consolidated in new subdivision (b)(3). - Probate Rules 5.670 and Part V — Florida Law Weekly lists an amendment issued 11-12-26; its substance and effective date were not obtained (see Could not verify). - Sharma impact: Sharma's contract and collections litigation runs on these rules. Two practical points: (1) from Jan 1, 2027, a "motion for rehearing" aimed at a non-final order is the wrong vehicle — calendar post-judgment deadlines against final judgments/orders only, and preserve issues per new (b)(3); (2) for contracts with arbitration clauses, the Rule 1.820 change is effective now — a missed trial-de-novo deadline is fatal to rejecting an award. - Sources (secondary trackers reproducing/linking the official opinions; flagged as such): https://www.flcourttracker.com/cases/in-re-amendments-to-florida-rule-of-civil-procedure-1-820-sc2026-0040.html ; https://www.flcourttracker.com/cases/in-re-amendments-to-florida-rule-of-civil-procedure-1-530-florida-smal-sc2025-1795.html ; https://www.floridalawweekly.com/flwonline/?page=rulerevisions

4.3 CONTEXT ONLY, NOT IN WINDOW — Coastal Building Contractors v. Calixte (Fla. 4th DCA, Nov 4, 2026)

The most Sharma-relevant lien decision in reach is dated November 4, 2026 and is therefore not in-window news; it is included only because it construes the exact deadline Sharma lives by. In Coastal Building Contractors v. Calixte, Nos. 4D2025-2593 and 4D2025-3611, the Fourth District held that a claim of lien recorded within 90 days of the lienor's actual final furnishing of labour/materials is timely under Ch. 713 even though the lien misstated the completion date (the lien said Oct 21, 2022; actual final furnishing was ~Dec 1, 2022). Whether the misstatement prejudiced the owner under the §713.08(3)/(4)(a) safe harbours is for the trial court on remand, as is the $80,000 fee award. Practice point: a wrong date on the lien is not automatically fatal, but the 90-day clock runs from actual final furnishing — Sharma's field logs, not its invoice dates, are the proof. Official opinion (primary): https://flcourts-media.ccplatform.net/content/download/2497681/opinion/Opinion_2025-2593.pdf
