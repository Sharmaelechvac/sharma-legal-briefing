  • What happened: A passenger was injured when the motorcycle fishtailed and ran off the road; there was no collision, debris, intoxication, or mechanical defect, and the plaintiff's own expert conceded a passenger's movement can cause loss of control and that motorcycles can crash without rider error. 2E. Fourth DCA: Boberg v. Ox of South Florida, Inc. — broad arbitration clause does not capture a negligence slip-and-fall claim — Thu Nov 12, 2026 (wider window)

A homeowner hired a company to coat the floor around his pool; the contract disclaimed any slip-resistant surface, disclaimed slip-and-fall injuries, limited tort liability, and gave the company an option to arbitrate anything “arising out of or relating to” the agreement.

Sharma impact — Sharma's residential/light-commercial service agreements likely contain the same kind of broad arbitration language.

Read the full story
  • What happened: A homeowner hired a company to coat the floor around his pool; the contract disclaimed any slip-resistant surface, disclaimed slip-and-fall injuries, limited tort liability, and gave the company an option to arbitrate anything “arising out of or relating to” the agreement. He slipped and sued. The trial court sent everything to arbitration. The Fourth DCA (per curiam) reversed as to the negligence count: under Seifert, even a broad clause requires a contractual nexus, and the duty not to negligently injure an invitee is imposed by law, not created by the contract — the contract's references to slipperiness did not change the source of the duty.
  • Sharma impact — HIGH for contract drafting: Sharma's residential/light-commercial service agreements likely contain the same kind of broad arbitration language. After Boberg, that language may not keep a personal-injury negligence claim out of court, however broadly it is worded. Do not budget or insure on the assumption that injury claims will be arbitrated; instead, treat the clause as effective for contract/payment disputes, keep the negligence exposure managed through safety, warnings, and insurance, and have Florida counsel review any clause that purports to disclaim slip-and-fall liability.
