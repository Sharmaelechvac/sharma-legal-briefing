1.1 FOLLOW-UP — Army detonates lead azide in Iowa, Wed Nov 25, ~9:00 AM CST — outcome: reported success, zero airborne lead detected so far

  • What happened: Two booms at about 9:00 AM CST on Wednesday, November 25, 2026 destroyed all 68 barrels of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, after the Eighth Circuit lifted the stay on the EPA permit on Nov 23. Maj. Gen. Eric Shirley, who led the operation, said at a press conference about two hours later that all the lead azide was destroyed and the operation was "tremendously successful." Acting Secretary of the Army Adam Telle said the detonation "exceeded all expectations" and the threat of a spontaneous detonation is eliminated. EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin reported an initial reading of 0.0 µg/m³ of lead; EPA's monitor map showed no lead on any monitor in the projected plume area as of 1:30 PM Wednesday. Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds issued a statement the same day confirming "zero detection of airborne lead in the region" and that state agencies will keep monitoring air, soil and water "in the days ahead." Middletown reported no known broken windows or structural damage.
  • Why it matters: This closes the emergency-permit litigation chapter (for now) and is a live example of how courts weigh a certain, mitigated environmental harm against a risk of uncontrolled harm — the Eighth Circuit's reasoning. Long-term soil and water sampling results are not yet in, so follow-on cleanup or tort litigation is still possible.
  • Effect on Sharma: No direct Florida effect. The practical lessons are generic but real: (i) degraded hazardous material in storage becomes an emergency that regulators, not the owner, end up timing; (ii) Sharma's own shops store refrigerants, solvents, batteries and fuel — inventory and disposal discipline is what keeps a routine compliance issue from becoming a forced, public one.

1.2 Federal judge blocks second birthright-citizenship executive order — Nathan v. Trump, Wed Nov 25, 2026

  • What happened: On Wednesday, November 25, 2026, U.S. District Judge Christopher R. Cooper (D.D.C.), in Nathan, et al. v. Trump, No. 1:26-cv-03377 (CRC), certified a class and granted a classwide preliminary injunction barring DHS, the State Department and the Social Security Administration from enforcing Executive Order 14418, "Continuing to Protect the Meaning and Value of American Citizenship," 91 Fed. Reg. 51991 (2026), against class members. The August 2026 order — issued after the Supreme Court's June 2026 decision in Trump v. Barbara struck down the first order — would have withheld recognition of citizenship from additional categories of U.S.-born children, including children of foreign-government and international-organization workers without full diplomatic immunity, children born through commercial surrogacy, and children with a parent suspected of gang or terrorist membership.   • Why it matters: A second executive attempt to narrow the Citizenship Clause by order has now been stopped at the preliminary stage on the ground that Barbara already foreclosed it. Expect a government appeal; the injunction holds while the case proceeds.
  • Effect on Sharma: Indirect. It does not change Form I-9 or E-Verify duties — citizenship status of employees' children is not an employer document issue, and Sharma must not ask for documents beyond what I-9 requires. It does signal continued volatility in immigration-adjacent executive action, which is the area where Sharma's hiring paperwork risk actually sits.

1.4 Executive Order 14439 published in the Federal Register Wed Nov 25 — new duties for federal contractors and subcontractors on congressional requests

  • What happened: Executive Order 14439, "Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting," was signed November 20, 2026 and published in the Federal Register on Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (91 FR 76627–76629, Doc. 2026-24114). It orders that all federal contractors must "immediately": (a) notify the White House and the contracting agency of any pending or future congressional request respecting the contractor's communications or work with the executive branch, and (b) abide by White House/agency instructions on withholding communications determined to be protected by executive privilege. Agencies must promptly inform contractors and subcontractors of these obligations; OMB must develop compliance guidance (no deadline stated). If a contractor or subcontractor fails to comply, agencies are to consider enforcement up to termination/cancellation of the contract, suspension, or debarment.
  • Why it matters: It extends an executive-privilege coordination regime to private companies by contract, with debarment as the stated stick. Its limits are statutory — the order by its terms operates consistently with applicable law and cannot itself override a valid subpoena or court ruling — but the compliance duty is framed as immediate.
  • Effect on Sharma: Only if Sharma performs work as a federal contractor or as a subcontractor on a federal job (e.g., electrical/HVAC sub work on a federal facility through a GC). If so: route any congressional request for records or testimony to the contracting agency/prime before responding, and expect flow-down notice from primes. For Sharma's ordinary builder and private work, no effect.
  • Source (primary): Federal Register, Nov 25, 2026 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/25/2026-24114/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting (read via the web index; the FederalRegister.gov fetcher presented a bot-check page, which was not completed — the document number, citation and text are as indexed from the official document.)

3.1 DOL proposes rewriting the ERISA retirement-plan investment rule — published in the Federal Register Tue Nov 24, 2026; comments due Jan 25, 2027

  • What happened: The Department of Labor's Employee Benefits Security Administration published a proposed rule in the Federal Register on Tuesday, November 24, 2026 — "Loyalty and the Exclusive Purpose Rule in Selecting Plan Investments and Exercising Shareholder Rights," RIN 1210-AC37, FR Doc. 2026-23936, amending 29 CFR 2550.404a-1 (released by DOL on Nov 23). It would: (i) require plan fiduciaries to act with the exclusive purpose of maximizing participants' retirement income or financial benefits (replacing the 2022 rule's "may not subordinate" formulation); (ii) rescind the 2022 "tiebreaker" test and the participant-preference provision; (iii) delete the sentence stating that risk-and-return factors may include the economic effects of climate change and other ESG factors; and (iv) tighten the rules on proxy voting and shareholder engagement for ESG/DEI purposes. Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling: the proposal "makes clear that retirement plan fiduciaries must make investment decisions solely in the financial interests of workers and retirees." DOL also states the proposal, if finalized, would apply prospectively but would not immunize fiduciary actions taken before its effectiveness.
  • Dates: Proposal only — the 2022 rule remains in force. DOL's fact sheet says comments are due 60 days after Federal Register publication; secondary reporting of the Federal Register document puts the deadline at Monday, January 25, 2027 (one trade source says January 23, the Saturday — use January 25 as reported from the document, and file early).
  • Effect on Sharma: Only if Sharma sponsors a 401(k) or other ERISA plan. Sharma does not pick funds itself in most small-plan setups — its recordkeeper/adviser does — but the plan fiduciary duty is the sponsor's. Practical step: when the plan's adviser next proposes a menu change, ask which standard it is applying (2022 rule vs. this proposal) and keep the answer in the plan file. No immediate action or filing is required.
  • Sources: DOL/EBSA fact sheet, Nov 23, 2026 (primary) — https://beta.dol.gov/research-data/fact-sheets/notice-proposed-rulemaking-loyalty · Barron's, Nov 24–25, 2026 (secondary) — https://www.barrons.com/advisor/articles/labor-department-esg-retirement-plan-rule-proposal-ad02f13d · Law-firm analysis (JD Supra / DLA-type alert), Nov 25, 2026 (secondary) — https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/dol-serves-up-a-new-proposed-erisa-esg-5790165/

3.2 FTC v. SHEIN — $100 million proposed consumer-protection settlement, Tue Nov 24, 2026

  • What happened: On Tuesday, November 24, 2026, the FTC filed a complaint and a proposed stipulated order in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California (No. 2:26-cv-13701) against SHEIN Distribution Corp., SHEIN US Services LLC and SHEIN Technology LLC. Under the proposed order SHEIN will pay $100 million in consumer redress, due within 30 days after a district judge signs the order — it is not yet in force, the Commission vote was 2–0, and the defendants neither admit nor deny the allegations. The FTC alleges violations of FTC Act § 5, the Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (shipping promises and delay notices), and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (suppressing negative reviews), plus deceptive supply-chain and cotton-origin claims and making it unreasonably difficult for customers to resolve purchase problems.
  • Why it matters: The three legal theories — say a ship/service date, miss it, and mishandle the notice/refund; filter out bad reviews; make claims you can't substantiate — are the standard FTC (and FDUTPA) playbook against businesses of any size, and the $100M figure was driven by complaint volume (12,000+ complaints, per Reuters reporting of FTC records).
  • Effect on Sharma: Sharma is a service business, not an online retailer, but the analogues are direct: (i) quoted completion dates and "we'll be there Tuesday" promises in texts/emails are representations — document delays and notify customers in writing when dates slip; (ii) never gate, filter or incentivise reviews in a way that suppresses negative ones — the Reviews Rule applies to service companies too; (iii) "licensed, bonded, made-in-USA equipment" type claims in Sharma's own marketing need a substantiation file. Florida's FDUTPA gives the state AG and private plaintiffs the same theories (§2.2 and the excluded Leapfrog item show the AG using it).
  • Sources (primary): FTC press release, Nov 24, 2026 — https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/shein-pay-100-million-over-allegations-deceptive-unfair-practices · FTC case page (complaint and proposed order PDFs, timeline dated Nov 24, 2026) — https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/shein

3.4 FCC's TCPA opt-out rules take effect Wed Nov 25, 2026 — new options for business texts and calls

  • What happened: The FCC's revised consent-revocation rules (FCC 26-67, adopted September 30, 2026; published at 91 FR 67214 on October 26, 2026) took effect Wednesday, November 25, 2026. From that date a sender may (i) designate one or more specified methods as the exclusive way to revoke consent, provided every call or text discloses that method, and (ii) treat a customer's opt-out from one category of informational messages as applying only to that category — the FCC's own example is that opting out of payment reminders need not stop fraud alerts, appointment reminders or outage notices the customer still wants. The FCC states the order imposes no new compliance obligation; these are options, not duties. Separately, comments on the FCC's companion further proposal (including shortening the 10-business-day opt-out processing deadline) were due November 25, 2026, with reply comments due December 28, 2026 — those changes are proposals only.
  • Effect on Sharma: Sharma texts customers appointment reminders, ETAs and payment reminders — exactly the FCC's use case. If Sharma's texting platform lets it designate an exclusive opt-out method, doing so (with the disclosure in every message) reduces the "customer claims they opted out by replying STOP to a different thread" dispute. Nothing must change today; do not adopt category-limited opt-outs without updating the disclosure language at the same time.

4.2 Florida Building Code 9th Edition transition — City of Miami Bulletin 2026-003 dated Mon Nov 23 [72-hr widen]; plan-acceptance cut-off Dec 30, 2026

  • What happened: The 9th Edition (2026) Florida Building Code takes effect December 31, 2026.   • What happened: America's Car-Mart, Inc., the Rogers, Arkansas used-car retailer and subprime auto lender, and five subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on Tuesday, November 24, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas (Case No. 26-90834).
