1.1 — BIGGEST STORY — Fifth Circuit rules 9–8 that the House unconstitutionally passed the 2022 spending bill by proxy voting — Friday, Nov 20, 2026.

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, ruled Friday, Nov 20 that the U.S. House of Representatives unconstitutionally passed the $1.7 trillion government funding bill in December 2022 through the pandemic-era proxy-voting rule,

Sharma impact — Do not misread this as the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act falling. As reported, the injunction runs only against enforcement against Texas (as an employer).

Read the full story
  • What happened: The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, ruled Friday, Nov 20 that the U.S. House of Representatives unconstitutionally passed the $1.7 trillion government funding bill in December 2022 through the pandemic-era proxy-voting rule, and voted 9–8 to uphold an injunction that outgoing Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton had secured. The bill passed the House 225–201, with 226 members voting by proxy under the rule Speaker Nancy Pelosi implemented in May 2020 (the rule was dropped when Republicans took the House in January 2023). Writing for the majority, Circuit Judge Cory Wilson held the proxy rule violated the Constitution's Quorum Clause, which the court said requires a majority of House members to be physically present to pass legislation: "While the chambers of the legislature have much discretion regarding their rules of procedure, the Quorum Clause's constraints on congressional power cannot be circumvented by congressional fiat." Circuit Judge James Graves, dissenting, called the ruling "illogical, inexplicable, and invalid," saying the majority read a physical-presence requirement into the clause and gave no explanation for limiting relief to one provision if the whole law was unconstitutionally passed. The relief is narrow: while the reasoning concerns how the entire bill was passed, the injunction upheld bars only the Department of Justice and the EEOC from enforcing the bill's Pregnant Workers Fairness Act provision against Texas. The DOJ, which defended the law, did not comment in the reporting located.
  • Why it matters: It is the first appellate holding that an enacted federal statute was passed unconstitutionally for want of a physical quorum — a ruling whose logic reaches every law passed by proxy in 2020–2022, even though the judgment itself touches only Texas and one provision. A further appeal (rehearing or Supreme Court) is the obvious next step; none was reported by the cutoff.
  • Sharma impact: Do not misread this as the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act falling. As reported, the injunction runs only against enforcement against Texas (as an employer). The PWFA's accommodation duties for private employers — including Sharma, for pregnant workers' known limitations — are unchanged by this ruling. The item matters to Sharma as constitutional background and as a warning against over-reading headlines: the statute under attack is one Sharma must still follow.
  • Source: Reuters (Nate Raymond), Nov 20, 2026, via SRN News — http://srnnews.com/us-house-unconstitutionally-passed-spending-bill-in-2022-court-rules/

U.S. District Judge Mary McElroy (District of Rhode Island, Providence) ruled Friday that HUD unlawfully attempted to overhaul the $4 billion Continuum of Care homelessness program by steering funding away from permanent housing toward transitional housing and supportive services.

Sharma impact — None direct — Sharma holds no HUD grants. Noted for the pattern, which is the same one that governs the grant and contract conditions that do reach contractors (§1.2, §3.1 of recent editions).

Read the full story
  • What happened: U.S. District Judge Mary McElroy (District of Rhode Island, Providence) ruled Friday that HUD unlawfully attempted to overhaul the $4 billion Continuum of Care homelessness program by steering funding away from permanent housing toward transitional housing and supportive services. HUD's plan would have set aside $1.3 billion of the fiscal 2026 appropriation for transitional and services-only grants. McElroy held HUD exceeded its authority under the McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act: Congress's delegation to create additional award criteria "cannot be read as allowing HUD to nullify the other congressionally established mandatory factors," including evaluating existing projects' performance and moving people into permanent housing. She also struck down a restriction on grantees' drug-user outreach activities (such as needle exchanges) and a "self-sufficiency" disqualifier, and ordered HUD to let applicants amend and resubmit applications through December 1 and to award funding by December 31. It is the latest round in a multi-year fight: McElroy blocked an earlier version in 2025 and again earlier this year, and a federal appeals court lifted one of those orders before 22 mostly Democratic-led states, D.C., and housing groups renewed the challenge on different grounds. HUD did not comment in the report.
  • Why it matters: Another agency-action limit with the same shape as §1.1's theme and yesterday's Ryan White ruling (§1.3 of the Nov 20 edition): an agency's residual discretion does not let it rewrite the mandatory terms Congress wrote into a funding statute.
  • Sharma impact: None direct — Sharma holds no HUD grants. Noted for the pattern, which is the same one that governs the grant and contract conditions that do reach contractors (§1.2, §3.1 of recent editions).

Sharma impact — None. Florida criminal-justice background only.

Read the full story
  • What happened: Circuit Judge Joseph Bulone (Pinellas County) on Friday afternoon, Nov 20 sentenced former plastic surgeon Tomasz Kosowski, 47, to death for the March 21, 2023 murder of Largo attorney Steven Cozzi, whose body was never recovered. Kosowski had represented himself, dismissed his court-appointed attorneys, waived a jury trial, presented no defense case and no mitigation, and declined to present a penalty-phase case; the judge appointed independent special counsel to present mitigation (his upbringing as a Polish immigrant, his education, and his potential to help other inmates); Bulone acknowledged the mitigating factors but found them outweighed — particularly by the aggravator that the homicide was "cold, calculated, and premeditated." At the hearing's start Kosowski moved to disqualify Bulone, who adjourned briefly and returned without addressing the motion, per CNN's account.
  • Why it matters: A rare self-represented capital bench trial ending in a judicial death sentence — the kind of waiver record (counsel, jury, mitigation all waived) that Florida's appellate courts scrutinize hardest on the automatic direct appeal that follows every Florida death sentence.

  • What happened: The Federal Trade Commission filed an amended complaint on Friday, Nov 20 adding ten states — Delaware, Georgia, Kentucky, Maine, Minnesota, New Mexico, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Carolina, and South Dakota — as co-plaintiffs in its deceptive-pricing suit against Dollar General Corporation, bringing the plaintiff states to 30.
