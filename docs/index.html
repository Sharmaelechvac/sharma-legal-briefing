  • What happened: U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes (D.D.C.) issued a 30-page temporary restraining order on Saturday, Nov 14, 2026, in the DC Preservation League litigation, enjoining the government from closing East Potomac Park and the East Potomac Golf Course during their Nov 14 public hours, through Nov 28, 2026 or further order. The park was due to close Sunday, Nov 15 for a planned 15-month redevelopment into a championship course. Reyes found the administration's stated closure reasons (safety, staffing, expiring operator lease) "pretextual" and that the government violated at least two federal laws "to meet an arbitrary deadline." The order does not require the government to operate golf, and repairs remain permitted. On Sunday, Nov 15, President Trump denounced the ruling on Truth Social and said the government would "immediately appeal, on an expedited basis" — Reuters notes TROs are generally not subject to appeal. DOJ said Saturday that planning may continue and the order does not preclude limiting access where the public is at risk.
  • Why it matters generally: A court stopped a federal construction/redevelopment project hours before mobilization because the agency's paper rationale did not match its real one — a pointed administrative-law / environmental-law lesson that stated reasons, notice, and process are litigable, and that a principal's own public posts can become evidence against the agency.
  • Sharma impact: Indirect but practical. Sharma does no federal park work, but the same pattern hits Florida public and builder work: a project shut down on the eve of start over defective justification/process. If Sharma ever bids public-sector or federally funded electrical/HVAC work, expect stop-work exposure where the owner's approvals, environmental review, or notice are shaky — price and schedule contracts accordingly, and do not mobilize on verbal assurances that approvals are "handled."

1.2 — Second Circuit sets aside FMCSA's $73.5M penalty against New York over immigrant commercial driver's licenses — decided Friday, Nov 13, 2026 (wider window; not in prior briefings).

In State of New York v. U.S. Department of Transportation, No. 26-1097 (2d Cir.

Sharma impact — Direct, in two ways. (a) Commercial vehicles: Sharma's service vans/trucks and any CDL drivers sit under the same FMCSA regime.

Read the full story
  • What happened: In State of New York v. U.S. Department of Transportation, No. 26-1097 (2d Cir. Nov 13, 2026; Nathan, J.), the court granted New York's petition and set aside FMCSA's final determination withholding 4% ($73,502,543) of New York's FY2027 federal-aid highway funds. FMCSA had penalized New York for issuing nondomiciled CDLs with 5-/8-year validity periods not matched to drivers' lawful-presence paperwork. The court held the determination "arbitrary, capricious, or contrary to law": as FMCSA itself conceded, the regulations in force at the time contained no expiration-matching requirement and no rule requiring cancellation when status changed — the agency could not penalize noncompliance with a requirement that did not exist (fair-notice principles). The court expressly did not rule on FMCSA's newer final rule (effective Mar 16, 2026, restricting nondomiciled CDLs to H-2A, H-2B and E-2 holders with explicit expiration-matching), which is challenged separately in the D.C. Circuit.
  • Why it matters generally: A clean post-Loper Bright-era administrative-law holding: agencies must rulemake prospectively; they cannot retrofit requirements onto regulated parties and then fine them.
  • Sharma impact: Direct, in two ways. (a) Commercial vehicles: Sharma's service vans/trucks and any CDL drivers sit under the same FMCSA regime. The ruling does not loosen current CDL eligibility — the stricter March 2026 nondomiciled-CDL rule remains in force and unadjudicated here — so do not read this as permission to relax driver-qualification files. (b) Immigration/workforce: the case is a reminder that a driver's license validity and work authorization are now actively audited at the federal level; if any Sharma tech or helper drives on a nondomiciled CDL, verify expiration-matching against immigration documents now, under the new rule, not the old one.
  • Source: Primary — opinion PDF hosted by the New York Attorney General, Case 26-1097, DktEntry 57.1, decided Nov 13, 2026 — https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/decisions/new-york-v-united-states-department-of-transportation-order-2026.pdf ; corroborating reputable reporting, Bloomberg Law, Nov 13 — https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/ny-prevails-in-noncitizen-commercial-license-clash-with-trump

2.1 — Koppert v. Homeowners Choice: 2nd DCA reverses three insurer-favorable judgments because an arbitration decision buried in a QuickBooks invoice email was never "served" — Friday, Nov 13, 2026 (wider window; NOT among the Nov 13 Florida decisions covered in the Nov 14 briefing).

Timothy Koppert, as personal representative of the Estate of Eldon Koppert v. Homeowners Choice Property & Casualty Insurance Co., Nos. 2D2025-0106, 2D2025-0124, 2D2025-0125 (consolidated) (Fla. 2d DCA Nov 13, 2026; Silberman, J.).

Sharma impact — Concrete and near-term. Sharma's disputes (builder payment fights, supplier claims, warranty/insurance claims on storm work) routinely pass through mediation/arbitration and email service.

Read the full story
  • What happened: Timothy Koppert, as personal representative of the Estate of Eldon Koppert v. Homeowners Choice Property & Casualty Insurance Co., Nos. 2D2025-0106, 2D2025-0124, 2D2025-0125 (consolidated) (Fla. 2d DCA Nov 13, 2026; Silberman, J.). Three consolidated Sarasota County cases (two hurricane-damage breach-of-contract suits and a negligence suit over a roof tarp) went to nonbinding arbitration, which the insurer won. The arbitrator emailed the decision on Sept 16, 2024 as an attachment to a QuickBooks billing invoice, subject line "New Invoice from Nicholas Mediation – Invoice 2024.0565P…". The trial court treated the insured's trial demand as late and entered judgment for the insurer. The 2nd DCA reversed and remanded, calling it a novel issue: under Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.820(h) the 20-day window to demand a trial de novo runs from service of the decision; service by email must comply with Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516(b)(2)(C), whose subject line must begin "SERVICE OF COURT DOCUMENT." It did not, so the clock never started, and the demand filed the same day the insured actually received the decision from opposing counsel (Oct 14) was timely. Section 44.103(5)'s "presented in writing" language did not save the insurer.
  • Why it matters generally: In Florida, a deadline can be won or lost on an email subject line. Informal delivery — even actual receipt buried in a billing email — is not service, and a judgment entered on a blown deadline will be reversed.
  • Sharma impact: Concrete and near-term. Sharma's disputes (builder payment fights, supplier claims, warranty/insurance claims on storm work) routinely pass through mediation/arbitration and email service. Two rules for the office: (1) when Sharma sends anything that starts a clock — notices, demands, arbitration decisions, lien-related papers — serve it exactly as the applicable rule requires and keep the proof; sloppy service may mean the clock never ran against the other side. (2) When Sharma receives a decision in an odd format, do not assume a deadline has or hasn't run — calendar from proper service and get counsel to confirm, exactly as saved the insured here.
  • Source: Primary — official 2nd DCA opinion PDF (Florida Courts) — https://flcourts-media.ccplatform.net/content/download/2498128/opinion/Opinion_2025-0124.pdf ; summary/index (secondary) — https://www.flcourttracker.com/cases/koppert-v-homeowners-choice-property-casualty-insurance-co-2d2025-0124.html
